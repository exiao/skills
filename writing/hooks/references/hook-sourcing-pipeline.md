# Hook Sourcing Pipeline (4 Steps to Never Writing a Hook From Scratch)

Source: @themariaines / Social Growth Engineers, "4 Steps To Writing Viral Hooks", Jul 24 2026. https://x.com/themariaines/status/2080640679496003834

Extends the [Moneyball Method](the-moneyball-method-for-hooks-social-growth-engineers.md), which says *source, don't invent*. This is the sourcing pipeline itself: four supply lines, ranked from fastest to most original.

| Step | Supply line | Speed | Originality |
|------|-------------|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Competitor hook replication | Fastest | Lowest |
| 2 | Niche influencer + comment mining | Medium | Medium |
| 3 | Pain point → audience-language search | Medium | High |
| 4 | Open prompts (audience writes them for you) | Slowest | Highest |

Run all four in parallel. Step 1 fills the bank today; step 4 refills it when you run dry.

---

## 1. Competitor Hook Replication (including cross-language)

Identify direct competitors, take their proven hooks as a base, ship your version. This is not a creativity failure, it is how you test proven angles without paying for the discovery.

**The cross-language arbitrage is the underused version.** A hook that peaked in one language is *unspent* in another. The audience there has never seen it.

Proven case:
- Roamy (EN): `i could LITERALLY KISS the flight attendant that showed me this` → 9.5M views, Nov 2025
- Latera (ES): `Podría LITERALMENTE casarme con la azafata que me mostró esto` → also went viral

Same structure, same emotional beat, one translation, second viral run.

**Rules:**
- Translate the *structure and emotion*, not the words. `kiss` → `casarme` (marry) because the literal translation landed weaker in Spanish. A machine translation of a hook is a dead hook; the idiom has to be native.
- Check the hook has not already saturated the target language. Search it there first.
- Applies across any boundary the audience does not cross: language, country, platform, or vertical. A hook that killed on TikTok fitness is unspent on Instagram finance.

**Bloom applications:**
- Pull the top hooks from the ES/PT fintech app accounts nobody in US finance TikTok watches, port them to English.
- Take a proven hook from a *different vertical* with the same emotion (fitness "I was doing this wrong for 3 years") and port it to portfolios.

## 2. Mining Niche Influencer Content for Recurring Problems

Study **non-brand** influencer accounts that post heavily in your vertical. Not competitor brand accounts, actual creators. Read what they talk about, how audiences react, and above all the comment sections.

The output is not hooks. The output is **clusters of recurring user problems** you then convert into hooks.

**The validation rule (this is the whole discipline):** a problem only counts when it *repeats across multiple independent accounts*. One creator's viral complaint is a fluke. The same frustration surfacing under five different creators is a real tension in the market, and a hook built on it will land.

Proven case (StudyTok): the recurring frustration was *fake productivity*, aesthetic study setups that produce no actual learning. A creator turned that tension into a hook that pulled 2M views. Tomo AI later adapted the same format for the app.

**Recycling:** hooks that went viral months earlier still perform when reused. Do not be afraid to reintroduce an old banger.

> Reconciling with the 1-2 week shelf life rule elsewhere in this skill: **trend-dependent hooks expire, pain-point hooks recycle.** A hook riding a specific sound or meme format (see Trend-Hijack Defection) dies with the trend. A hook built on a durable human frustration ("I study for hours and retain nothing") has no expiry, only audience overlap. Recycle the second kind freely; kill the first kind on schedule.

**Bloom applications:**
- Mine finance-adjacent creators (not brokerage accounts): personal finance TikTok, FIRE YouTube, /r/investing power posters. Look for the frustration that shows up under all of them.
- Candidate durable clusters: "I don't know if I'm actually doing well or just riding the market", "every stock take online is someone's bag", "I bought and immediately felt stupid".

## 3. Pain Point First, Then Adapt to Audience Language

Write the hook straight from a known user pain point, then either search for a matching proven format or test it immediately.

**Adapting to the target audience's language is critical**, and this is where most brand hooks die. The pain is right, the words are corporate.

**The habit that supplies this:** 30 minutes a day on TikTok, searching inside one vertical, saving hooks. A curated gallery of inspiration. This is a standing daily practice, not a pre-launch research sprint. The gallery is the asset.

Proven case (TLDL, 105M views): took a recurring student pain point, validated it in comment sections, and shipped:
`when you're too tired to study but you can't sleep 'cause you need to study` → 18.6M views, 3,668 relatable comments.

Structure: **proven viral format → pain point hook → caption app promo.** The pain point supplies the words, the borrowed format supplies the reach, the app never appears until the caption.

The 3,668 "this is literally me" comments are the signal that the pain point was real. That comment texture is itself the validation metric, not the view count.

**Bloom applications:**
- `when you finally check your portfolio but you already know it's bad so you close the app`
- `when everyone says buy the dip but you already bought three dips ago`
- Put the app in the caption, not the hook.

## 4. Open Prompts (Make the Audience Write Your Hooks)

When you run out of angles, stop writing and ask. Posts that request honest opinions, stories, or experiences generate comment sections full of phrasing, tensions, and problem statements you can turn directly into hooks.

Proven case (college-advice niche): `I'm bored comment ur most INSANE freshman year story. Not just "we got caught by the RAs" like actually unhinged.` → 545.9K views, 1,718 comments. One AI image, one hook, and a whole library of genuine stories from the audience.

**Why the prompt is built the way it is:**
- `I'm bored` = zero-stakes, casual, not a brand asking for engagement
- `most INSANE` = superlative forces people to reach for a real story, not a generic one
- `Not just "we got caught by the RAs"` = **pre-banning the obvious answer is the whole trick.** It raises the floor on quality and signals you already know the cliché, so responses skip it. Same mechanic as a negation listicle (`5 X that aren't Y`), applied to solicitation instead of delivery.

**Rules:**
- Ban the obvious answer explicitly, by quoting it.
- Ask for a story or a tension, never an opinion on your product. "What do you think of X" gets you nothing usable.
- The post is a hook-mining instrument that also happens to perform. Treat the comments as the deliverable.
- Harvest verbatim. The awkward specificity is the value; sanitizing it produces the same generic hook you were stuck on.

**Bloom applications:**
- `comment the dumbest stock you ever bought. not "I bought the top" like actually embarrassing.`
- `what's the worst investing advice someone confidently gave you? not "buy low sell high", real ones.`
- `tell me the stock you're still holding out of pure spite`

## Feeding the Hook Bank

All four steps write into the same artifact: a live hook bank of 15-20 tested hooks, updated weekly. Never store a naked sentence. Store the package:

- the hook
- persona it worked for
- channel it performed on
- proof mechanism that made it credible
- conversion evidence (views vs. actual installs)
- **source snippet** (the competitor post, the comment, the influencer video) so a winner can be traced back to the language that created it
- **origin step (1-4)**, so you can tell whether the bank is over-indexed on cheap replication and starved of original pain-point work
